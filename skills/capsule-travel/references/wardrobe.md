# Wardrobe and outfit images

Identify visible garments and shoes, linking each to an attachment or the user's description. When a photo contains several pieces, record which ones the user intends to take. Reuse short item IDs so corrections don't silently change an outfit. Separate owned items, planned purchases, and your suggestions. Do not assume that the user owns a suggested accessory or automatically add purchases.

Describe color, cut and length when visible. Treat warmth, waterproofing, comfort and fabric composition as unknown unless supplied by the user or supported by a product source. Ask about shoe comfort and weather protection when they affect the plan. Never assume that clothing determines someone's gender or that a pictured model is the user.

Build varied combinations for the planned activities. Repeat versatile basics while varying layers or tops; do not use an identical outfit every day by default. Consider laundry, garment reuse, luggage and the user's preferences when relevant. Separate missing items from the confirmed packing list. If the wardrobe is incomplete, propose a useful partial plan and identify the gap.

## Consistent visual treatment

Use the host's image-editing or image-generation tool only if it supports the supplied photos as references. The default is a square, top-down flat lay on a warm off-white background (#F5F3EE), softly lit with gentle shadows, spacing between complete garments, and no text, frames or numbering inside the image. Keep the style consistent across outfits unless the user prefers another presentation.

Supply the actual attachments and an explicit list of selected items to the image tool. Never reconstruct the user's clothes from a generic text prompt while claiming they came from the photos. A useful editing brief:

> Compose only these selected items from the supplied photographs: [items and photo references]. Remove people, hangers and unrelated backgrounds. Arrange the garments and shoes as a top-down flat lay on a warm off-white background with soft light and subtle shadows. Keep each item fully visible without overlap. Preserve the main color, distinctive pattern, cut, length and item count. Minor changes to folds, seams and texture are acceptable. Add no garments, jewelry, branding or text.

If a substantial part of an item is hidden and its shape is uncertain, ask for another photo or omit it with explanation. Don't infer the unseen garment. After generation, inspect the result against the originals. Fix missing or additional pieces and major color, shape or length errors. Minor detail changes do not require endless regeneration or repeated approval.

Caption the first image briefly as a visualization from the user's photos; avoid repeating the caveat with every image. Show the outfit name and one line connecting its pieces to an activity. This is a flat lay, not a guarantee of fit or an exact product photograph. If the composition changes, update its image too.

Without reference-image editing, use a collage of actual attachments if possible, clearly named as a collage; otherwise show combinations in text. Don't substitute unrelated stock photos. Preserve the user's original photos and don't publish them as examples.
